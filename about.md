---
title: Cognitive Scaffolding
line: Tools for structured overthinking, so the thinking ends in something done.
rule: Every run ends in an intervention, not a conclusion.
---

I overthink. Give me a problem and I'll run it through philosophy, realpolitik, critical theory, the state of the world, the science and the social science, and at the end I'll understand the problem beautifully and be sitting alone in a room. Intellectualised rumination is a very good way to withdraw while feeling like work.

So this is scaffolding. It doesn't cure overthinking. It's a container for it: a fence on the time, a small set of reasoning tools, six lenses with guards on them, and one rule. Every run ends in an intervention, not a conclusion. The intervention is a thing I'll do, with a when, that moves toward a person, the body, or the world off the screen.

The bet is old psychology. We infer who we are from what we watch ourselves do. Log enough interventions and the next time the loop starts, it starts in a different person, one who does interventions.

AI is allowed in, on terms. It can ask. It can't answer for me. The sparring brief tells a model to question me, and it forbids the model from writing the intervention. Augmentation should leave me more capable, not replaced.

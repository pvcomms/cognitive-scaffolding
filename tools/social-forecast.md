Two findings aim straight at withdrawal. Train commuters asked to talk to a stranger predicted it would be unpleasant. In fact it made the ride more pleasant than sitting alone, and they underestimated how interested the other person would be.

After a first conversation, people consistently underestimate how much their partner liked them. This is the liking gap, and among new college suitemates it lasted for months. The prediction that keeps you home is reliably wrong, and wrong in the same direction every time.

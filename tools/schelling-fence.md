Thomas Schelling wrote about self-command. The self that sets a rule and the self that wants to break it act like two negotiators, and the one that commits first wins. A Schelling fence (Scott Alexander's term) is a bright, somewhat arbitrary line that holds because everyone agreed on it, not because it's the perfect place. Any line you argued your way to, you can argue your way past.

Worry-control research uses the same shape. You set a fixed time and place for worrying, and worry that comes up outside it gets postponed until then.

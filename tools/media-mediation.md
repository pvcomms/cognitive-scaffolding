Technologies don't just carry perception. They shape it, making some things louder and others quieter. Ihde's postphenomenology calls this mediation. Feeds amplify along a known axis: in one large study, each moral-emotional word in a post raised its spread by about a fifth. And repetition alone makes a claim feel truer. That's the illusory truth effect.

A belief assembled from a feed can still be accurate. But part of its urgency belongs to the channel, not the world.

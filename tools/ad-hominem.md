---
name: Ad hominem, turned inward
family: argument
order: 1
when: You're dismissing a plan because of who's making it, and the who is you.
move: Separate the claim from the claimant. Judge the plan as if a stranger had proposed it.
ask: Strip out who's saying it. What is the claim, and does it hold on its own?
---
The textbook ad hominem attacks the arguer instead of the argument. Rumination runs it on yourself: "this won't work, because I'm the kind of person who doesn't follow through." That uses a fact about your history as a verdict on a plan. Your record is evidence about base rates. It is not a refutation.

Run it outward too. An idea from someone you dislike isn't wrong for that reason, and one from someone you admire isn't right for it. Cognitive therapy calls the inward version *labelling*.

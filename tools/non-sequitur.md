---
name: Non sequitur
family: argument
order: 2
when: The conclusion is far bigger than the evidence. One awkward evening became "I should stop going out."
move: Write the premise and the conclusion on a line each, then name the step that would have to sit between them.
ask: Premise, then conclusion. What would have to be true to get from one to the other?
---
A non sequitur is a conclusion that doesn't follow. Rumination makes them quickly, because the mood supplies the missing premise without saying so. Getting from "that went badly" to "people don't want me around" needs "one evening stands for all evenings" in the middle. Said out loud, the hidden premise usually doesn't hold up.

Cognitive therapy's names for the everyday versions are *overgeneralisation* and *jumping to conclusions*.
